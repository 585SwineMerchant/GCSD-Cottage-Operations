# Curriculum alignment and boundary

The source curriculum documents support an event-centered production system. They do not require this application to become a student information system.

## What the operational packet supports

| Curriculum theme | Version-two operational support |
|---|---|
| Workplace communication and teamwork | Generic team/station assignments, instructions, deadlines, and handoffs |
| Kitchen organization and mise en place | Equipment lists, production quantities, and station-specific work |
| Food safety and sanitation | Event-level safety controls, allergen controls, quality checks, and teacher-approved instructions |
| Culinary mathematics and costing | Menu quantities now; scaling, market orders, and costing remain later phases |
| Menu planning and service | Event type, service format, customer commitment, menu, location, and guest count |
| Work-based learning and pop-up events | Request intake, private draft, controlled publication, production packet, and generated Event Order |
| Professional reflection and assessment | Remains in Google Classroom or other district-approved academic records |

The initial skill vocabulary can be drawn from the supplied scope-and-sequence, employability profile, and pathway skills map: food safety, equipment, knife skills, culinary math, nutrition, menu planning, marketing, baking, produce, grains, eggs and dairy, proteins, stocks and sauces, sustainability, global cuisine, communication, resource management, and quality of work.

## Deliberate privacy boundary

The teacher may publish an **event-level learning focus**, such as `communication, culinary math, and quality control`. The system must not attach competencies, ratings, attendance, performance comments, grades, certifications, or individual roles to a named student.

Individual employability ratings—including attendance, punctuality, workplace appearance, quality of work, resource management, communication, problem solving, safety, and technical proficiency—remain in the district's approved student-record systems. They are reference material for instructional design, not fields in the Cottage Operations public or operational model.

## Terminology notes for later review

- Use `ServSafe` when referring to the certification unless the district specifies a different credential name.
- Use `HACCP` for Hazard Analysis Critical Control Point.
- Allergen guidance must be reviewed against current district and regulatory requirements before production use; curriculum examples are not a substitute for an approved food-safety plan.
- The next data-model phase should use stable competency codes rather than copying long rubric text into event records.
